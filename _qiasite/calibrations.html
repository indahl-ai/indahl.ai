What can be corrected, and what each correction returns.

A calibration here is applied to a workload and measured against an uncorrected reference in the same job. Each row says which finding it addresses, what it returned on hardware, and its status. Combinations are measured only after each part reads on its own.

Result on hardware

Null control: with the same data and the table scrambled across qubits, the correction gave back more than half the kick in 0 of 6,000 draws on ibm_kingston and ibm_marrakesh (exact 95% upper bound 0.12%), so the measured table, not any correction, does the work.

Echo on the control qubit during idles on a coupled pairSpin echo for static coupling is standard practice

D1/R5Demonstrated on hardware, 17 Nov 2026 (ibm_fez)
Your run: without / with
Coherence an idling qubit loses over 30 us beside its coupled partner, as compiled / with the echo (ibm_fez 13-14, 17 Nov 2026): 59% / 46%. Where the coupling is weak (ibm_fez 39-53) the echo changes little: 43% / 41%. The echo recovers the coupling share of the loss; the rest is ordinary dephasing, which this correction does not address.
Cost
no extra QPU time to apply; about 3 minutes to measure the coupling first
Result on hardware

17 Nov 2026, ibm_fez, repeated run: an echo pulse on the control qubit returned about 13 percentage points of coherence on edge 13-14 (loss 59% [56, 63] as compiled, 46% [42, 50] with the echo) and left edge 39-53 about unchanged (43% [39, 46], 41% [37, 44]). On an uncoupled control pair the echo neither added nor cost coherence (0.977 [0.934, 1.019]), so the gain is the coupling removed. The measured coupling on those edges is 0.76× and 0.34× of the published value.

95% intervals on the losses; an uncoupled control pair read in the same job

Your run: without / with
Bell pairs held through repeated readouts of neighboring qubits, each arm against an idle of the same length on the same pairs (17–18 Nov 2026; readout corrected with the same job’s calibration). Loss under the readouts without our calibrations / with the echo alone: ibm_kingston -0.253 [-0.412, -0.111] / -0.010 [-0.026, +0.001]; ibm_fez -0.056 [-0.100, -0.017] / -0.011 [-0.029, +0.001]; ibm_marrakesh -0.083 [-0.240, +0.040] / -0.020 [-0.067, +0.004]. Share of the loss given back (uncorrected counts): phase correction alone 0.96–0.98 on ibm_kingston, 0.83–0.97 on ibm_fez; echo alone 0.95–0.96 on ibm_kingston, 0.74–0.82 on ibm_fez (ranges span the two placements; 99% intervals in the results files). With IBM’s readout figures as published, these lines read cannot-tell: the record flags 3 ibm_kingston and 9 ibm_fez qubits in these pairs.
Cost
about 450 s per processor
Result on hardware

17–18 Nov 2026, ibm_kingston and ibm_fez, all arms interleaved in one job per chip, five trials: the phase correction alone gives back +0.22 (ibm_kingston) and +0.05 (ibm_fez) of pair fidelity; the echo alone +0.36 and +0.21 (all ABOVE, every trial on the same side). The echo figure also includes the echo protecting the pair during the idle itself, so the two figures are not a ranking.
