Quantum kernels: how hard they fade, and what it costs

A machine-learning research team that trains quantum-kernel classifiers on IBM hardware. Before they scale up, they need to know how much of their kernel survives the chip and how many shots that will cost.

What the record can't tell you

IBM's own record can't pin how hard your kernel fades at scale. Read with gate errors only, it predicts too little fade. Read with its decoherence times, it predicts far too much. At 40 qubits the two readings are 50 times apart. One job of about 23 seconds per chip measures it.

Self-score is the kernel's score of a point against itself. The ideal circuit gives 1 at any size. Higher is better. 99% intervals per size are in each chip's analysis file.

On ibm_kingston at 40 qubits, IBM's record puts the self-score anywhere from 0.005 (decoherence reading) to 0.257 (gate-only reading). We measured 0.079. The record brackets the real fade, but too widely to plan a run on.

Readout audit

We also checked readout on the qubits the client's own layout picks, against IBM's matching one-way figure for each qubit. The clearest case: one qubit on ibm_marrakesh misread a 0 as a 1 about one time in 20, in both runs. IBM's matching figure says one in 1,000.

The fade repeated in direction at every size on every chip. It also moved: ibm_marrakesh at 8 qubits went from 0.736 to 0.691, outside run 1's 99% interval (0.726 to 0.747). On ibm_marrakesh the fade moved within a day, so measure it the day you run.

What we changed

Nothing in the client's circuit. One short job reads the fade the chip actually delivers at the client's own sizes, on the client's own layout, and prices the shots from that reading instead of from the record.

How it was checked

  • Hardware only: every number comes from runs on ibm_kingston, ibm_fez and ibm_marrakesh. No simulated result is quoted.
  • The plan and the reading from IBM's record were committed before the run.
  • IBM's automatic changes were off on the measuring job and read back: no gate or measurement twirling, no dynamical decoupling, no error mitigation.
  • The same plan ran twice, with fresh jobs and nothing tuned between runs.
  • Readout compares like with like: the measured one-way error against IBM's one-way figure for the same qubit.

Outcome. Measured self-score against the reading written from IBM's record before the run (readout times gate fade): BELOW at every size on every chip, in both runs (30 of 30). Against the gate-only reading: BELOW. Against the all-decoherence reading: ABOVE. Both bounds are printed.

99% intervals per size and chip are in each run's analysis file. Example: ibm_marrakesh, 8 qubits, run 1: 0.726 to 0.747.
