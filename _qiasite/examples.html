Worked example (from our record)

Mid-circuit readouts on IBM processors

For teams running mid-circuit measurements: error correction, dynamic circuits, feed-forward.

What we checked
The turn a mid-circuit readout gives the phase of qubits that are not read, per qubit, on ibm_fez, ibm_kingston and ibm_marrakesh (17 November 2026).
What came back
Turn per readout, with 39 qubits read at once: 1.75 to 2.51 degrees on the three processors, same direction on all three and on both dates measured (99% intervals in the source).
What the client gets
A per-qubit correction table measured minutes before the job (it returned 86 to 93% of the turn; in 6,000 random shuffles of the table across ibm_kingston and ibm_marrakesh, none returned more than half), an echo where the circuit puts pairs beside a readout, and whether to re-measure: a 27-day-old table still returned 82 and 90% on two processors, and on ibm_marrakesh it had drifted by about a degree.

Source: phase correction, combinations, finding E1.

Worked example (from our record)

What an old readout snapshot does to a correction

For anyone who corrects readout with published figures (readout mitigation).

What we checked
IBM’s published readout figures for ibm_fez qubit 155 against our own measurement of the qubit, and what a correction built from each does to an entangled pair read on it (17 to 18 November 2026).
What came back
IBM’s record stated P(1|0) = 0.982. Correcting with that figure divides by 0.007, and the pair read fidelity 26 and 72 (impossible values; 1 is the maximum). Our readout guard flags any qubit whose published figures leave less than half the signal and refuses to correct with them; corrected with the same job’s calibration, the pair read normally.
What the client gets
A list of the qubits in your circuit whose published readout figures fall outside the guard, and the same-job calibration to use instead.

Source: readout correction, finding R10.

Worked example (from our record)

Monitoring the same processors over time

For teams that depend on the same processors week after week.

What we checked
Each qubit’s readout and T1 measured against the record in force on a regular cadence (129 runs in eight days), with a signed receipt per run; and, once, the per-qubit phase table re-measured against its 27-day-old copy.
What came back
Our regular runs saw bad-readout episodes on ibm_marrakesh qubits 64 and 76 about 4.4 and 2.5 hours before IBM’s refresh of 13 November recorded them. The phase table had drifted by about one degree in four weeks, and the old table overshot by the amount predicted before the run.
What the client gets
A reading of your processors on a fixed cadence, signed receipts you can hand to a third party, and a flag when a correction table needs re-measuring. Any qubit can be read live through the free Test Drive.

Source: finding R10, phase correction.
