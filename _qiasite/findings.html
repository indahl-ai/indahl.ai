Published properties unchanged for days; a snapshot of an intermittent qubit held in place

R10
IBM’s figure
backend.properties(): readout entries normally refresh about daily, all 156 qubits together; ibm_fez q155 prob_meas1_prep0 = 0.982 (readout_error 0.497), not in IBM’s faulty-qubit list; target measure error on (155,) 0.497
Measured
Measured on ibm_fez q155, 17–18 Nov 2026: P(1|0) 0.010 to 0.014. Read 18 Nov 2026, about 12:00 UTC: ibm_phoenix’s published properties are unchanged since 13 Nov UTC. From 13 Nov until 18 Nov 08:46–09:46 UTC, ibm_fez, ibm_kingston, ibm_boston and ibm_pittsburgh published nothing new; their newest records carry 18 Nov stamps, but every readout entry is still the old one (ibm_fez 9 Nov, ibm_kingston and ibm_pittsburgh 12 Nov, ibm_boston 13 Nov) and most T1 entries likewise. From 18 Nov 03:51 UTC, ibm_marrakesh published again (new initialization errors and a few gate entries first), and it refreshed its readout and T1 entries at 18 Nov 08:21 UTC; ibm_chicago kept publishing (newest record 18 Nov 05:09 UTC), but only its readout entries are new (dated 18 Nov, except P(0|1) on 7 of 120 qubits still 10 Nov); its T1 and T2 entries date from 10 Nov. The readout entries of ibm_fez are dated 9 Nov 23:54 UTC. Qubit 155 on ibm_fez enters real bad-readout episodes lasting hours (15 of our 205 regular calibration runs since 30 Oct, 7%). IBM’s last readout refresh before the record stopped, 9 Nov 23:54 UTC, caught the end of one: it states P(1|0) = 0.982; our regular runs three and one hours earlier (20:54 and 22:54 UTC) had already read 0.936 and 0.995, and our next run, about one hour after the refresh (00:57 UTC), read 0.008. Since then the record has kept that snapshot. Under it, from our first run after IBM’s refresh (10 Nov 00:57 UTC) to 18 Nov 03:52 UTC, 117 of our 129 regular runs read P(1|0) 0.003–0.023, 3 read 0.03–0.11, and 9 were further episodes (0.21–0.86). On ibm_marrakesh, q64 and q76 follow the same pattern (IBM’s refresh at 13 Nov 03:47 UTC states 0.497 and 0.467; our regular runs had read q64 at 0.21–0.44 from 23:24 UTC and q76 at 0.52–0.79 from 01:19 UTC, both still elevated 21 minutes after the refresh and back near their usual values within hours; good in about 95% of hours since). The 18 Nov 08:21 UTC refresh of ibm_marrakesh replaced both: q64 P(1|0) 0.002, q76 0.096. Laid out from the published target, an 80-qubit GHZ circuit leaves q155 out (5 of 5 seeds); with the measure error of q155 set to its usual value it uses it (5 of 5). Avoiding a qubit with bad episodes is not clearly a harm, so this shows a layout change only.
Date and interval
18 Nov 2026, about 12:00 UTC (records read); 17–18 Nov 2026 (measured)
99% Clopper-Pearson on 8,192 shots per prepared state (readout run, 17 Nov) and 12,500 (in-session calibration of the combination run, 18 Nov)
How to check

IBM: from qiskit_ibm_runtime import QiskitRuntimeService; b = QiskitRuntimeService().backend('ibm_fez'); p = b.properties(); print(p.last_update_date, p.qubit_property(155)['prob_meas1_prep0'], b.target['measure'][(155,)].error)

Ours: About 2 seconds of QPU on your own account: prepare |0> on qubit 155 and measure, 4,096 shots (QuantumCircuit(156, 1); qc.measure(155, 0); transpile with initial_layout=[155] is implied by the physical index), then count how often it reads 1 and compare with prob_meas1_prep0 above. Same for |1> (add qc.x(155) first) against prob_meas0_prep1. Our raw counts and scripts are available on request.
