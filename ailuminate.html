Division: IRIS

Do the published results stay what they say?

MLCommons publishes percentile scores for AI systems on its AILuminate Safety benchmark, with a Detailed Results report for each system. It states that the reference set is fixed so that adding systems does not shift the scores of those already tested. The results page and every report are read, the page is compared with its own reports, and every read is kept so that any later change to a published score shows up.

Loading.

Source: mlcommons.org/ailuminate/safety/results/ and the public folder of Detailed Results it links. The page is checked against the reports. The band is 0 to 15 percent, open at 15, and the size that matters is 5 percent. A share of disagreements is CONSISTENT when the whole 95 percent interval sits inside the band and does not reach both 0 and 5 percent, ABOVE when the lower end is at or above 15 percent, and otherwise cannot tell, with the reason beside it. With 27 systems, 0 disagreements gives an interval that reaches both 0 and 5 percent, so it reads cannot tell (TOO_WIDE_TO_RESOLVE). This rule is in a plan addendum dated 12 November 2026, committed before any reading under it. Published scores kept: a system is changed when any of its 13 percentiles or its release month differs from the first value stored. MLCommons states that the overall score weights all 12 categories equally. The table of overall against category percentiles sets each overall percentile beside the mean of its 12 category percentiles. Plan, notes and code.

Versions and the evaluator

MLCommons labels every result with its benchmark version, prompt set and evaluator version. Its release notes say it will not change what a score means without changing the label, and that results under an earlier label are not comparable. They also announce evaluator updates and margins of error for November and December under the current label.

Scores under a label, 0 NOTHING RETURNED. CANNOT TELL 1 of 1 (1 TOO_WIDE_TO_RESOLVE). How scores move between labels, item by item, and whether results under an earlier label stay published: these read cannot tell (NO_INTERVAL_FORMABLE) until a second label appears, and then run on their own.
