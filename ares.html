Do public conflict trackers agree on where armed violence happened?

Two public trackers record organized armed violence around the world. The Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP) codes an event as “An incident where armed force was used by an organized actor against another organized actor, or against civilians”, with at least one registered death at a specific location and a specific date. The GDELT Project codes events from news text by machine, and its Material Conflict class holds the CAMEO root codes for assault, fight and unconventional mass violence.

This page asks two questions for every province and month in the window. Do the trackers agree that organized armed violence happened there? Where they disagree, which tracker recorded it? Each is counted, not explained. A province-month is one first-level administrative unit, such as a province, state, oblast or governorate, in one calendar month.

The frame, fixed before any data was read

The window is the trailing twelve complete calendar months that ended at least 30 days before the run, October 2025 to September 2026 on this run. UCDP counts a province-month when it holds at least one event of any of its three types, placed at least to the province (where_prec 1 to 4), and in Candidate data with the status Clear. The types are pooled and never printed apart. UCDP GED version 26.1 is read for the months it covers and the Candidate release for the rest, GED first where both exist, since GED is the checked release. GDELT counts a province-month when it holds at least one root event with QuadClass 4 and root code 18, 19 or 20, placed at a world city or a first-order division (ActionGeo_Type 4 or 5), seen in at least two sources. Country-level matches are dropped, since they sit on a centroid.

Places are grouped into provinces through one public first-level boundary file, Natural Earth Admin 1 at 1:10m, which is never drawn. A crosswalk from UCDP’s first-order names and GDELT’s FIPS 10-4 province codes to that file was sealed before any province-month was counted. A name or code the crosswalk cannot match is held and listed by its source spelling, never guessed.

Dot size is active days: the number of distinct days in the month with at least one UCDP event placed in the province and dated to the exact day. It is not a human-cost figure, it runs from none to every day of the month on every map, and one long day of reporting counts as one day. GDELT days are not used for size, since duplicate stories inflate them.

The map

Solid dot: both trackers agree. Hollow dot: one tracker only. Dot area follows active days, from none to every day of the month. Hover a dot for the province as the source spells it, the month, which trackers, and the active days. No borders or country fills are drawn.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

UCDP’s line is drawn by deaths: an event enters UCDP only if at least one death is registered. This page reads that as yes or no and never reads, stores or prints the number. GDELT’s line is drawn by news reports, and Hammond and Weidmann (2014) found that “at the local level major issues of geographic bias exist in how events are reported.” As the filter is written, GDELT is read outside the United States only (ActionGeo_Type 4 or 5). Candidate events are preliminary and can change in the yearly GED release.

Left out on purpose: every count of people harmed, every name of a party or side, displacement figures, flags, borders, country fills, coordinates and towns. The finest place on this page is the first-order unit. Place names are quoted as each source spells them. ACLED is left out on its license terms and its account requirement.

A reading says where the trackers agree that violence was recorded. It does not say why they differ.
