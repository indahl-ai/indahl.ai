Do dogs of a breed behave the way its standard says?

A breed standard describes the temperament a breed club wants. The words are adjectives, not levels, and some of them state a direction, such as friendly or not aggressive. This page reads those words against the open Darwin’s Ark release behind Morrill and colleagues, “Ancestry-inclusive dog genomics challenges popular breed stereotypes”, Science 2022. In that study owners answered short surveys on their dogs’ behavior, and the authors reduced the answers to behavior factors and estimated each dog’s breed ancestry from its genome. The paper’s own summary is that “Breed offers little predictive value for individuals, explaining just 9% of variation in behavior.”

A dog counts as a member of a breed when at least 85 percent of its genetic ancestry comes from that breed, by the release’s own estimates. A breed is read when at least 25 dogs meet that rule and its standard can be read. Each phrase of the standard’s Temperament section was mapped to one of the 8 scored behavior factors and a direction before any behavior data was opened. A phrase with no matching factor is counted apart and never forced. The mapping was read before the first run, and its changes are dated in the plan addendum. For each mapped statement the breed’s mean factor score is set against the mean of all other dogs in the release, with a 95 percent interval from 2,000 bootstrap draws over dogs. Each breed, factor and direction is counted once in the headline, and restated phrases are listed beside it. All known-good checks passed on this run.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

Behavior is owner-reported, not observed. Dogs in the release are those whose owners joined, which is not a sample of all dogs. A standard describes the breed as its club wants it, which may be read as an ideal rather than a claim about every dog, and this page still reads the words as written. Only breeds with at least 25 dogs at 85 percent ancestry or more are read, so most standards are not. The interval covers sampling of the dogs in the release and not how they came to join. A reading says where a breed’s average sits. It does not say why.
