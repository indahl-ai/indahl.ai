CASSANDRA · Warnings against what followed

Division: HERMES

PRELIMINARY: first run, not refereed Every share on this page counts floods reported, not floods.

When a flash flood warning goes out, does a flood report follow?

The National Weather Service directive on hydrologic products, NWSI 10-922, says: “Flash flood warnings are issued when flooding is imminent or likely.” Each warning carries its own polygon and its own expiry time, so each one states a place and a window. This page counts every flash flood warning issued from 2008 to 2025, hits and misses alike, and asks whether a flash flood report followed inside the polygon before the warning expired. No warning is chosen.

The directive gives “likely” no number. This page reads it at its weakest plain meaning, more often than not, a share of 50 percent. That reading is the page’s own, fixed in the plan before any data was read, and not the National Weather Service’s. The share is a measurement first. The reading against 50 percent sits beside it.

The outcome is a report, not a flood. A flood in an empty place may go unreported and counts as a miss. Reporting effort may rise while a warning is out, which can raise the share. Neither effect is measured here. All known-good checks passed on this run.

The headline cell

Every flash flood warning in the window against the NCEI Storm Events record, the official record of flash flood events. A warning counts as followed when at least one flash flood event began inside its issuance polygon at or after the issue time and at or before the final expiry, extensions with the same event number included.

Held years are left out and never filled in. Warnings whose polygon or time could not be read: 0. Rows for the same warning more than once in the file, the earliest kept: 36. What this page holds and what it leaves out

Held: every storm-based flash flood warning in the archive issued from 1 January 2008 to 31 December 2025, in any state or territory, each with its issuance polygon held to the final expiry. Polygons redrawn in follow-up statements are not read. Left out: flash flood watches, areal flood warnings, river flood warnings and warnings before 2008. Warning polygons before October 2007 were experimental, and the window starts after them. A reading says how often a report followed a warning. It does not say why.
