Division: HERMES

Is the price file where the rule says it is?

The federal hospital price transparency rule asks each hospital to publish a machine-readable file of its standard charges, and to put a small text file named cms-hpt.txt at the root of its website that links to it. For every hospital website listed in Wikidata, three things are checked in order: the text file at the root, whether the file it links to opens as data, and whether that file starts with the template header names.

Loading.

Wikidata lists clinics and parts of health systems, and a hospital that belongs to a system can have its file at the system’s domain, so a missing file at a hospital’s own domain can be correct. The full read of ten rows is in the plan. For that reason the first step reads CANNOT TELL with the reason NO_INTERVAL_FORMABLE, because the share cannot be formed from a population that holds non-subjects. Steps two and three are scored against a claim of zero disagreement. ABOVE when the lower end of the 95 percent interval is at or above 5 percent, CONSISTENT when the whole interval lies below 5 percent, and CANNOT TELL otherwise with a reason beside it. A share of 10 percent is the size that matters, so an interval that holds both zero and 10 percent reads TOO_WIDE_TO_RESOLVE, and so does a step with fewer than 30 websites compared. The rule moved to the Heuremen Standard on 12 November 2026 by a plan addendum, and earlier readings stand under the rule they named. REQ-13 beside each step gives the websites that returned nothing, which sit outside the share, and the share of CANNOT TELL by reason. Websites that could not be opened, that refused the user agent, or that are a page on a shared site are counted apart. JSON and zip files are not checked for the header because their key names can sit after the data. Every failing row of steps two and three that has been read in full is listed with what the read found. Failing rows not yet read in full are counted above the tables as not read in full.
