Division: HERMES

Stated launch time against the time it flew

Every upcoming orbital launch carries a stated target time that keeps being revised. The schedule source keeps only the current time, so it is read repeatedly, each change is stored, and a launch is scored after it flies. For each launch, the time stated 1, 7 and 30 days before it flew is taken and how far the flown time was from it is measured.

The first snapshot was taken on 28 October 2026 and earlier history is not claimed. Rows appear here as launches already being watched fly. Rules were committed before the first snapshot.

Slip is the absolute difference in hours. Above: the lower end of the 95 percent interval of the share that slipped by more than 24 hours is at or above 10 percent. Consistent: the whole interval lies below 10 percent. Cannot tell comes with a reason beside it. TOO_WIDE_TO_RESOLVE means fewer than 30 launches are scored or the interval holds both zero and 25 percent, which is the size that matters. STRADDLES EDGE means the interval overlaps the 10 percent edge. NO_INTERVAL_FORMABLE means no interval can be formed. The same rule applies at every lead. It moved to the Heuremen Standard on 12 November 2026 by a plan addendum, and earlier readings stand under the rule they named. REQ-13 beside each row gives the list reads that returned nothing and the share of cannot tell by reason. Data: Launch Library 2.
