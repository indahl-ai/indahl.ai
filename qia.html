QIA · Quantum Idle Audit

Check a Run

The IBM record in force at the start of a run, any record stamped during it, the flags for its qubits, the indahl.ai measurement of those qubits, and any time IBM’s calibration was off near the run. Runs in the browser. Nothing is sent or uploaded.

Loading.
The four checks

STALE means T1 or T2 was measured more than 24 hours before the record’s stamp. ZERO means a readout figure (readout_error, prob_meas1_prep0 or prob_meas0_prep1) is exactly 0. T2>2T1 means a published T2 above twice the published T1, which a single-exponential T2 cannot reach. Above 2.5 times T1 reads ABOVE and between 2 and 2.5 reads CANNOT TELL, because IBM publishes no fit width and a band is needed. PAIR means T1 and T2 were measured more than 24 hours apart.

The same checks as qia_check.py in the QIA Research Package, with identical answers on the same records. A record IBM stamped after the last mirror read is not included.

    Rule changes

    Rules for the hourly calibration runs that changed after a reading was made, with the readings each change touched. The first entries stay in the chain, and each re-read is a new entry that names the entry it corrects.

    • 30 October 2026, about 02:20 UTC (Note 1). Set after the first ibm_aachen run was read and before any US run was read. IBM’s readout figures that are exact multiples of 1/4096 are counts from about 4,096 shots, so each gets its own exact 99.9% interval, and ABOVE or BELOW needs the two intervals not to overlap. IBM’s T1 is ABOVE only beyond 1.25 times the top of the measured interval and BELOW only under the bottom divided by 1.25. Readings touched: that ibm_aachen run, corrected by a new entry.
    • 30 October 2026, 13:57 UTC (Note 3). Set after the ibm_fez and ibm_marrakesh readings were published. Readout figures that are multiples of 1/1024 are read as counts from 1,024 shots. Readings touched: every ibm_fez and ibm_marrakesh run then in the chain, re-read from its saved raw counts and saved record, each published as /qia/cal/<job>n3.json beside the original.

    IBM’s calibration, measured when it changes

    One job on each IBM processor that can be reached whenever IBM publishes new readout or T1 figures, and at least every 6 hours: readout at rest, readout after an X, and T1 on every qubit, each set against the calibration IBM had in force when the job ran. Code and plan: qia-cal. Process.

    Calibration records against dated runs: the chain

    Every time one of the indahl.ai jobs measured a qubit against the calibration IBM published for it and found IBM’s figure outside what the qubit delivered, the reading goes here as one entry: processor, qubits, IBM’s figure and the date IBM gives for it, what was measured with its interval, the time the job ran, and the job id. Check a job: pick the processor, type the qubits and the time the job ran. Entries are hashed and chained, append only, so the record cannot change without showing it. Every calibration run above adds one entry too. Everything is free: the whole chain and the script that verifies it.

    The Quantum Idle Audit

    Every calibration record IBM publishes is checked against itself and against IBM’s other records. No QPU time is used. Assistants run the same audit as one tool, qia_audit, on the indahl.ai MCP server.

    A qubit cannot have T2 above twice its T1. The audit counts the qubits whose published T2 is above 2.5 × T1, and those between 2 and 2.5 × T1. IBM publishes no fit uncertainty for either figure, so each of these readings is CANNOT TELL (NO_INTERVAL_FORMABLE). It also compares each qubit’s T1 date with its T2 date, the age of every entry, and whether a restamped record changed any value.

    T1 and T2 can be measured together on any flagged qubit with an IBM account and the open-source check, which prints its cost and submits nothing until confirmed. Process.

    Stability over time

    Every saved record of each processor, compared with the others: how often T1 and T2 are measured again, how far apart their dates are, and which qubits are ever flagged. The plan was committed before the first table.

    T1 and T2 measured together

    For the qubits the audit flags, T1 and T2 were measured on the same qubit in one job on the processor, with a control qubit in the same job, and read the ratio against the physical limit of 2. The plan was committed before the results were read. Notes 3, 4 and 6 were written after some results were read. To rerun it: the check in the public repository.
