PRELIMINARY: first run, not refereed

When the Congressional Budget Office prints a two-thirds range years ahead, does the outcome land inside it?

Since August 2016 the Congressional Budget Office has printed ranges around some of its projections for the deficit, federal debt held by the public and average real GDP growth. Each range comes with the same words: “roughly a two-thirds chance” or “approximately a two-thirds chance” that the outcome falls between two numbers. CBO says it builds these ranges “based on historical” differences between its past baseline projections and the outcomes. If the ranges are sized as stated, about two in three outcomes should land inside.

This desk reads every such range CBO printed with two endpoints, in the report that first printed it, and sets it beside the outcome. The deficit and the debt are fiscal-year figures as CBO records them in its own evaluation data, divided by fiscal-year GDP. Growth is the calendar-year average annual growth of real GDP, from the first CBO data release after the period in which the whole period is actual, with the latest revised figure printed beside it. A range holds the outcome when the outcome, rounded to the decimals the range prints, lies between the two endpoints, endpoints included. The headline counts ranges whose target year is three or more years after the report. The interval comes from a bootstrap that draws whole reports, so ranges from one report stay together. The plan, with the frame, the headline cell and the outcome rule, was fixed and committed before any outcome was read, and the list of ranges was copied from the reports and committed before any actual was opened. All known-good checks passed on this run.

Three things travel with every result. CBO projects under current law, so a later change in law can move an outcome away from a projection made under the law of its day. CBO sizes its ranges from its own past record, so a share near two-thirds is partly built in over the years that record covers. Few reports print ranges, which widens the interval. Debt held by the public ended fiscal year 2024 at 97.4 percent of GDP and fiscal year 2025 at 99.4 percent of GDP. Of the 3 debt ranges three or more years ahead, 2 sat below the outcome.
