PRELIMINARY: first run, not refereed · 30-day window, late autumn · borrowed radius

When the Storm Prediction Center draws a thunder line, how often does lightning strike inside it?

Each Day 1 Convective Outlook from the NOAA Storm Prediction Center carries an unlabeled thunder line. The Center’s product page says the line “depicts, to the right of the line,” “a 10% or higher probability of thunderstorms during the valid period.” NWS Instruction 10-512 says the outlooks “define areas where there is a 10% or greater probability of” “(general) thunderstorms.” Neither names a radius for thunder. The only radius in either is for severe weather, 25 statute miles of a point. This page borrows that radius and prints a fixed second radius of 10 statute miles beside it.

The lightning is the flash record of the Geostationary Lightning Mapper on GOES-19 (east of 106.2 degrees west) and GOES-18 (west of it), from NOAA open data. NOAA states a 70 percent flash detection requirement and a measured 81 percent for GOES-18 in 2023, lower toward the edge of the view. No published figure for GOES-19 was found. A missed flash can only lower the share, so the share is a floor. GLM also counts all lightning it sees from orbit, in-cloud and cloud-to-ground alike, while a thunderstorm forecast is about thunder heard at the ground. That difference is stated here and not measured.

The outlook is the 1300 UTC issuance, valid from 1300 UTC to 1200 UTC the next day. The thunder area is the general thunder polygon with every higher category inside it. Points sit every 0.1 degree over the 48 contiguous states and the coastal waters within 25 statute miles of them, 89,987 points in all. A point counts as struck when at least one flash with the product’s good quality flag fell within the radius during the valid period. The interval comes from a bootstrap that draws whole outlook days, so points on one day stay together. The plan, with the frame, the headline cell and the outcome rule, was fixed and committed before any outlook was set beside a flash. All known-good checks passed on this run.

Reading: SPC states 10 percent or more, a floor. Consistent if the whole 95% interval is at or above 10 percent, below if the whole interval is under it, otherwise cannot tell with the reason. Above cannot occur against a floor. The share of flashes beyond the radius of every thunder area has no stated figure and carries no reading. A point-day is one grid point on one outlook day.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

Left out: the other Day 1 issuances, the Day 2 and Day 3 outlooks, the severe probabilities, and years before this window. A years-long reading would be a later addendum. A reading says how often lightning struck where the line was drawn. It does not say why.
